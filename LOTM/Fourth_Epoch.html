Fourth Epoch

The Epoch of the Gods—an age of endless war, rising and falling empires, the ambitions of tyrants, and the slow descent into the Pale Era.

Known as the Epoch of the Gods, the Fourth Epoch is the bloodiest and most chaotic age in human history. Born from the collapse of the Glorious Era, it is defined by constant warfare between the Orthodox Churches, the Solomon Empire's collapse, the rise of tyrannical emperors, the ambitions of the Four Horsemen, and the ever-growing desire for godhood. Throughout its entirety, the shadow of the Orthodox Gods looms over every conflict, with many seeking to usurp their thrones or replace them entirely.

Major spoilers for Lord of the Mysteries and related lore follow.

Overview

More commonly referred to as the Epoch of the Gods, the Fourth Epoch is an age of unrestrained ambition. With God Almighty absent, the Seven Orthodox Gods no longer content themselves with merely guiding humanity. Many sought to expand their own authority, whilst mortals, fueled by the same ambition, sought to ascend beyond humanity and claim divinity for themselves.

The Northern Continent is ripped apart by ceaseless war. Empires are forged overnight and crushed the next day. The title of "Emperor" is claimed by countless warlords, only for most to be drowned in a sea of blood. High-Sequence Beyonders become weapons of mass destruction, angelic wars are fought in the shadows of the material world, and the line between man and god grows thinner with each passing century. By its end, the continent is a broken husk, drained of life, faith, and hope.

Warring Era Years 0–112

The Fourth Epoch begins with the collapse of the Glorious Era. The delicate balance maintained by God Almighty shatters the moment the Seven Orthodox Gods turn against one another and their followers. What was once inter-Church rivalry devolves into total war across the entire continent.

The mighty Solomon Empire, already rotting from within, crumbles almost immediately. Its armies are pulled apart by civil war as provinces declare independence, nobles side with different Churches, and high-Sequence Beyonders carve out their own domains. The Orthodox Churches march under the banners of their gods, but their true goals are quickly revealed to be far more than just doctrinal supremacy. Each seeks to secure as much territory, as many Pathways, and as much authority as possible in the power vacuum left by God Almighty.

This era of unrestrained slaughter lasts for over a century. By its end, there is nothing left of the unified continent. Ashen battlefields stretch from horizon to horizon, old nations are wiped off the map, and the people of the Northern Continent are left broken and desperate for any form of order, no matter how tyrannical.

The Solomon Empire Years 112–429

Out of the fires of the Warring Era, a new Solomon Empire is established by the Solomon Dynasty. Claiming direct descent from the imperial family of the Third Epoch's Solomon Empire and carrying the banner of God Almighty, they rally the war-weary remnants of humanity under the promise of restoration, unity, and a return to the glory of old.

For a time, it appears as though the dynasty may succeed where all others had failed. Through a combination of unmatched military might, the backing of several Orthodox Churches, and the leadership of terrifyingly powerful Beyonders, the new Solomon Empire manages to unify large swathes of the Northern Continent once more. Roads are rebuilt, order is restored in conquered lands, and the Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun rises to immense prominence under imperial patronage.

However, history repeats itself. The Churches grow bolder with each passing year, refusing to be leashed by the throne. The nobility schemes incessantly, and the imperial family itself begins to be plagued by corruption and infighting. More importantly, many within the empire begin to fall victim to the same sin that brought down their predecessors: the desire for godhood. The seeds of rebellion are sown, and the empire slowly begins to rot from the inside once more.

The Tudor-Trunsoest United Empire Years 429–581

The Solomon Empire's second collapse gives way to one of the Fourth Epoch's most infamous periods. The House of Tudor and the House of Trunsoest, two of the most powerful noble houses, forge an unprecedented alliance through marriage and conquest, establishing the Tudor-Trunsoest United Empire.

This union would be defined by ambition on a scale never before seen. The Tudor family, in particular, would produce figures of terrifying talent and even more terrifying ambition. Most notable amongst them is Alista Tudor, who would rise through the ranks of mysticism at a blistering pace, eventually becoming known to history as the Blood Emperor.

Under the United Empire, war reaches new heights of brutality. The Blood Emperor begins his meteoric ascent, slaughtering his way up the Sequence ladder whilst amassing a cult-like following of fanatical supporters. The empire expands rapidly through conquest, swallowing up the territories of rival factions with terrifying efficiency. However, the union between the two great houses is always tenuous at best. Jealousy, paranoia, and the lust for the throne fester behind closed doors. It is only a matter of time before the empire turns on itself.

The United Empire would ultimately collapse under the weight of its own ambition, paving the way for yet another attempt at restoration.

The Second Solomon Empire Years 581–922

Once more, a branch of the Solomon Dynasty manages to reclaim power, establishing the Second Solomon Empire. Determined not to repeat the mistakes of their predecessors, the new emperors rule with an iron fist, attempting to curb the power of both the nobility and the Churches with draconian measures.

For a time, this iron rule brings about a period of brutal stability. However, the continent has changed far too much for such rule to last. The Churches have grown to be forces unto themselves, many powerful enough to challenge the empire directly. Countless secret orders dedicated to ascension have spread like weeds throughout the nobility and even within the imperial court itself.

The empire becomes increasingly obsessed with the supernatural. The pursuit of godhood is no longer considered heresy by the radical factions within it, but a goal to be achieved. Black magic, forbidden rituals, and dealings with entities best left alone become commonplace amongst the upper echelons. The line between worshipping the Orthodox Gods and attempting to replace them grows impossibly thin.

By the latter half of this era, the empire is a powder keg. Corruption is endemic, faith is fractured, and the most ambitious among the nobility are already plotting their own ascension to the throne of godhood. All that is needed is a spark to set the continent ablaze once more.

The War of the Four Emperors Years 922–979

The spark comes in the form of open rebellion. Four titans of the era, each a powerhouse of terrifying might and each harbouring the ambition to become a god, rise up to claim dominion over the Northern Continent. History would come to know this conflict as the War of the Four Emperors.

The four contenders are remembered as:

  • The Solomon Emperor – The last true representative of the Solomon Dynasty, desperately clinging onto the legacy of his forebears and the hope of restoring the old order.
  • The Tudor Emperor – A scion of House Tudor, carrying the burning ambition of the Blood Emperor within his bloodline. Many believe he seeks to complete what Alista Tudor started and claim the throne of God Almighty for himself.
  • The Trunsoest Emperor – The ruler of the Trunsoest faction, cold, calculating, and backed by immense wealth, resources, and a terrifying host of high-Sequence Beyonders.
  • The Black Emperor – The most mysterious and terrifying of the four. Wielding unfathomable power and leading an army of zealots, the Black Emperor marches with the explicit goal of overthrowing the entire Orthodox order and establishing a new, tyrannical rule of darkness.

For fifty-seven years, the four clash in a war of unprecedented scale. Entire cities are reduced to rubble, angelic battles rip open the sky, and the Spiritual World encroaches onto the material plane with every major clash. The Orthodox Churches are dragged into the conflict, forced to pick sides or be crushed in the crossfire. Entire Pathways are on the verge of becoming derelict as their Beyonders are slaughtered en masse.

By the end of the war, the Northern Continent is utterly devastated. The Second Solomon Empire is no more, the Tudor and Trunsoest factions are broken, and the Black Emperor stands triumphant over a sea of corpses. In the aftermath, the Black Emperor would establish his own reign, casting a long shadow over what is to come.

Conflicts of the Seven Gods Years 979–1192

With the mortals having slaughtered one another to exhaustion, the true powers of the era can no longer remain idle. The Conflicts of the Seven Gods erupts as the Orthodox Gods themselves turn on each other in an open, apocalyptic war for supremacy.

This is not a war fought solely through their clergy. Angels clash with Angels, divine kingdoms manifest in the Spirit World and Astral World, miracles are perverted into curses, and the very concept of faith is weaponized. Entire Churches are annihilated overnight, their holy sites razed, their gods' authority ripped away and seized by their rivals.

The war is marked by countless betrayals, broken alliances, and acts of unspeakable blasphemy. Some gods attempt to usurp the authority of others, others desperately cling onto their domains, and a select few begin plotting to take the place of God Almighty himself. The faith of humanity is shattered. Many turn away from the gods entirely, whilst others become fanatical worshippers of whichever god is currently slaughtering the rest, desperate for salvation in a world gone mad.

By the time the divine war comes to a halt, the Seven Orthodox Gods are all severely weakened. Their Churches lie in ruin, their authorities are fractured and contested, and the once unshakeable divine order is irrevocably broken. Never again would the Orthodox Gods be able to unite as they did during the Glorious Era. The age of divine unity is dead.

Pale Era Years 1192–1358

The final era of the Fourth Epoch, the Pale Era, is aptly named. The Northern Continent has been reduced to a desolate wasteland. Great forests have been turned to ash, mighty cities lie in ruin, fertile lands have become barren, and an ever-present grey pall hangs over the sky. Many regions are completely devoid of life. The people that remain live in fear, huddling within crumbling walls, clinging onto the scraps of faith that they have left.

With the Orthodox Gods too weak to enforce their will and the great empires all but destroyed, the continent descends into a state of feudal-like ruin. Warring lords, broken knight orders, heretical cults, and remnants of the ancient Churches all fight over the tiniest scraps of territory and resources. Mysticism is still practiced, but it is far more dangerous than ever before. The knowledge of the old age is being lost with every passing generation, locked away in crumbling libraries or hoarded by secretive orders on the brink of extinction.

Faith has grown cold and lifeless. Prayers go unanswered more often than not. The name of God Almighty is rarely spoken with genuine hope anymore, only as a distant memory of a glory that will never return. Whispers of the Outer Deities grow louder in the desolate lands, and the seal placed upon them during ages past begins to grow thin once more.

As the Pale Era draws to a close, the continent is quiet. An eerie, lifeless quiet. It is the calm before the storm. From the East, across the now-fractured world, the wheels of history begin to turn once more, heralding the coming of the Fifth Epoch—the modern age.
